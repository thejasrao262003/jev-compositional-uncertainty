# Pre-registration: human validation of the constructed target

**Status:** written 2026-10-23, before any rating exists. The sample and
packet are generated by `research/src/build_adjudication_packet.py`.
**No model call is involved.**

## Question

The paper's failure label is disagreement with a constructed target
action, a\* = F(reference regression, reference severity). How well does
a\* agree with what competent, independent evaluators judge to be the
right action? And where they disagree, which link is responsible:

- the **tracker labels**;
- the **rule F**;
- or a **genuine ambiguity**?

This is a validation **pilot** (n = 50). It is not definitive proof of
target validity, least of all for the rare escalate action. The
sampling method is preserved, and every estimate is reported with its
uncertainty, not as ground truth.

## Sample (fixed)

- **50 of the 500 held-out bugs:** 25 Mozilla and 25 kernel, drawn
  uniformly within each tracker with seed `zlib.crc32("adjudication")`.
- **The natural mix is kept.** It is not enriched for errors, so the
  result describes the target as it was used.

## What evaluators see, and do not see

- **They see** the cleaned bug text exactly as the models saw it (title
  plus description, after the published cleaning).
- **They do not see:**
  - reference labels (severity, regression, component);
  - the target action;
  - any model output or confidence;
  - whether any model counted the bug as a failure;
  - the tracker URL or bug ID. Bugs are shown under opaque codes, in
    random order.

## Evaluators

**Two evaluators, rating independently:** Tushar M and Thejas P Rao.
Both are software engineers who work with bug reports. Neither may
consult the trackers, the other's ratings or any model while rating.

**Disclosed limitation.** Thejas designed the study and has seen
aggregate results. He has not seen per-bug labels for the held-out set
in a way that could be recalled for 50 random bugs. This is stated, and
inter-rater agreement is reported separately for each rater.

## What each evaluator records per bug (rubric)

1. **Severity (0 / 1 / 2 / can't tell).** These are the same definitions
   the models were given:
   - **0** — cosmetic; no impact on functionality;
   - **1** — broken or degraded feature, but a workaround exists;
   - **2** — blocking; no workaround exists.
2. **Regression (yes / no / can't tell):** did something that used to
   work break?
3. **The right action (accept / defer / escalate / can't tell).** Judged
   directly, from these operational definitions, **without** applying any
   formula:
   - **escalate:** someone should look at this now; leaving it in the
     normal queue risks serious harm;
   - **defer:** a real problem that should be scheduled for the next
     release, but not dropped everything for;
   - **accept:** normal queue handling is appropriate.
4. **Free-text reason** (one line), required when the action differs
   from what the evaluator's own severity and regression would suggest.

## Procedure

1. Each evaluator completes their own CSV
   (`research/data/adjudication/ratings_<initials>.csv`) independently,
   and it is committed **before** the evaluators see each other's
   ratings.
2. **Disagreements** between the evaluators on the action are then
   discussed, and a consensus action is recorded with a reason (in
   `consensus.csv`). The independent ratings are never edited.
3. **Only then** is the key joined: codes to bug IDs, reference labels
   and a\*.

## Analyses (descriptive; Wilson 95% intervals; Cohen's κ with bootstrap CI)

- **Inter-rater agreement** on action, severity and regression (κ and
  raw agreement).
- **Target validity:** agreement of the consensus action with a\*, and
  of each rater's action with a\*.
- **Label validity:** agreement of the evaluators' severity and
  regression with the tracker's labels.
- **Rule validity:** agreement of F(evaluator severity, evaluator
  regression) with the evaluator's own direct action.
- **Error attribution.** Each a\* disagreement is classified as one of:
  - label-driven (the evaluator's severity or regression differs from the
    tracker's);
  - rule-driven (F of the evaluator's own judgments differs from their
    direct action);
  - ambiguity (can't tell, or the raters disagree).
- **Disagreement by action type:** whether mismatches with a\*
  concentrate in accept, defer or escalate. Escalate is rare in a natural
  50-bug sample, so its cell is reported with its count and is not
  interpreted alone.
- **Reasons for disagreement,** coded from the free-text reasons and the
  label comparison into:
  - label interpretation;
  - the mapping rule;
  - missing evidence in the text;
  - genuine ambiguity.
- **Failure-label check.** On the 50 bugs, how often Jev's "wrong action"
  label (a ≠ a\*) agrees with "wrong action" defined against the
  consensus action.

**"Can't tell"** is a category of its own and is excluded from agreement
denominators, with its rate reported.

## Interpretation (fixed now, descriptive)

- **No pass/fail threshold is set,** since this is a pilot.
- **The paper reports the agreement figures with their intervals.**
- **Strong qualification.** If consensus–a\* agreement is materially
  below the inter-rater agreement, the paper states that the headline
  metric measures an imperfect proxy for the decision of interest, and
  quotes the failure-label agreement.
- **Supportive evidence.** If the two are comparable, the paper states
  that the constructed target agrees with independent judgment about as
  well as two judges agree with each other.
- **Either way, the result is reported.**

## Amendment 1 (2026-10-24): AI-judge pilot, written before the key is opened

**What was received:** one set of action ratings for the 50 bugs,
produced by an AI assistant (`ratings_AI_pilot.tsv`, with rationales in
the session record). This is **not** the pre-registered two-human
validation, and it does not satisfy it.
- **One rater, and an AI.**
- **No severity or regression ratings,** so label-validity and
  rule-validity analyses are not possible.
- **It uses a category outside the rubric** (`request_evidence`).
- **Its "defer" means low urgency** (rationales such as "no build failure
  or runtime impact"). The rubric's defer means "schedule for the next
  release", which is more urgent than accept.

**Analysis (exploratory, labelled "AI-judge pilot"; mapping fixed now,
before the key is opened):**
- `accept` → accept; `escalate` → escalate.
- `request_evidence` → **can't tell**, excluded from agreement
  denominators, with its rate reported.
- **AI "defer":** reported under two pre-stated readings, with no choice
  made between them:
  - (R1) taken literally as defer;
  - (R2) read as accept, per its rationales.

**Reported:**
- agreement with a\* (Wilson 95%);
- Cohen's κ (bootstrap 95%);
- the confusion matrix by action;
- agreement of Jev's failure label (a ≠ a\*) with "a ≠ AI action", on the
  bugs the AI rated.

**The pre-registered human validation remains open.**
